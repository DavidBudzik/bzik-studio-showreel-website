The standard

Precise by dimension. No AI guesswork.

Generated images look convincing until someone measures them. Windows move, containers change length, stairs stop adding up. A buyer or an engineer notices, and trust goes with it.

Built from your drawings

  • Every container, opening, beam and stair tread is placed at its drawn dimension.e.g. 40 ft HC · 12 190 × 2 440 × 2 900

One model, every output

  • Renders, videos, drawings and the interactive view all come from the same 3D model, so they never disagree.

No generative images or video

  • No morphing, no invented details, no glitches. Every frame is rendered from real geometry.
The process

I handle the whole process

You send plans in any form: PDF, DWG or a dimensioned sketch. I take them through to finished marketing outputs. Example project: a two-storey house of five 40 ft High Cubes, about 149 m².

Step 01 · Drawings

It starts with the plan

Example set P2: 14 A3 sheets at 1:50, coordinated with the 3D model, plus two E1 engineering coordination sheets. When the design changed (a two-flight return stair, a relocated WC), the drawings were updated to match the model.

Click the sheet to zoom.
Step 02 · Module layout

Reading the plan as real modules

Each long wall in the plan is a container seam. Three 40 ft High Cubes sit side by side on piers (C1 north to C3 south), and two more stack on C1–C2. The roof of C3 becomes the terrace. Removing the long walls turns each box into a frame, so seam beams and posts pick up the load.

Step 03 · 3D model

One model, built in layers

  1. Base module: one 40 ft HC with corrugation and castings, instanced five times
  2. Openings + steel: 220 scheduled hollow-section members, each with its own ID
  3. Glazing: W01–W09, D01 pivot door, original red D02 doors
  4. Interiors: fitted furniture, bathrooms, PBR materials
  5. Lighting + site: 69 fixtures in 17 zones, open field planting
  6. Stair + rooms: 18-riser two-flight return stair, relocated WC and laundry
